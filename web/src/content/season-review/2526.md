## Two seasons, running side by side

The Premiership was effectively over by the end of September. The Conference changed
hands ten times and wasn't settled until the penultimate month. Same draft night, same
38 gameweeks, completely different sport.

## The draft, and the two men who took Isak

Haaland went first overall in the Conference to Henry Cartmell, and second in the
Premiership to Tom Shiel. He finished as the highest scorer in the game on 239. Both got
precisely what they thought they were getting, which happens less often than anyone
admits.

Isak went third in the Premiership to Joe Payne and fourth in the Conference to Ivan
Rutherford. He scored 41 points across the entire season. Two of the first four picks in
the whole competition returned, between them, roughly one good gameweek.

## August: everybody wants Marc Guéhi

The defining transaction of the season happened in the first month, in both leagues, and
almost everyone was involved. Henry Cartmell got Guéhi in [GW2](gw:2). Luc Baldwin got him
in the Premiership in [GW4](gw:4). Mike Nelson and Ivan Rutherford both went for him in
that same gameweek and were beaten to it; he went on to score 156 points from that point
on. Luc Baldwin's move was worth 126 points over the rest of the season, Henry Cartmell's
144. Nobody's second-favourite centre-half did anything comparable.

Andrew Newbery, meanwhile, opened his season by moving Rice out for Savinho in
[GW1](gw:1). Rice scored 184 points afterwards. It was, by a distance, the worst
individual decision made by anyone all year, and it was made before most of the group had
finished unpacking their first squad.

## The Premiership: a procession

Luc Durandt led for three weeks. Tom Shiel took over in [GW4](gw:4) and led for the next
thirty-five, which is every remaining week of the season. By [GW10](gw:10) he was 66
points clear. By [GW30](gw:30) it was 85. He peaked at 108 in front with a gameweek to go
and won by 102.

It is difficult to make a title race sound dramatic when the leader changes once, in
September. The interesting Premiership question was always what was happening at the
other end, and there the margin was six points. Mike Nelson finished on 1609 and Luc
Durandt on 1603, and both went down. Durandt had led the league in August, which is the
sort of arc that gets brought up at every subsequent draft night.

## The Conference: ten lead changes

Four different people led the Conference in the first eleven weeks — Henry Cartmell, Alex
Clark, Chris Roper and Billy Phillips took turns at it, mostly by accident. Alex Clark
settled into it from [GW14](gw:14) and held on for fourteen weeks. Chris Roper took it
back in [GW28](gw:28) and by [GW30](gw:30) was 47 points clear.

At [GW31](gw:31) Alex Clark was 50 points behind and the Conference looked finished. From
[GW33](gw:33) came 75, 73, 47 and 86 — a four-week run that retook the lead in
[GW36](gw:36) and turned a 50-point deficit into a 37-point win. Chris Roper went up as
runner-up, which is the correct outcome and no consolation whatsoever.

## Billy Phillips has the best and worst week of anyone

In [GW33](gw:33) Billy Phillips scored 93, the highest single gameweek by anyone in
either league all season, and a 53-point improvement on the week before. In
[GW34](gw:34) he scored 32. He finished bottom of the Conference on 1573 and wears the
chicken suit. Nobody in the group has had a wider range of outcomes in consecutive weeks
and less to show for it.

## Which league was better

The Conference won 22 gameweeks to the Premiership's 15, with one tied, and averaged
slightly more per manager across the season — 45.2 against 44.9. On that evidence the
Conference was the stronger division.

On the other hand, Tom Shiel's 1873 would have won the Conference as well, and Alex
Clark's 1845 would have finished second in the Premiership. The Conference was deeper.
The best team was in the Premiership. Both leagues can keep their preferred version.

## Points left on the bench

Every manager in the competition finished with a three-figure total of points left in
their own dugout. Ivan Rutherford led the way on 219, with Peter Vickers on 213; Luc
Baldwin topped the Premiership on 208. Alex Clark won the Conference having wasted 161,
and Tom Shiel won the Premiership having wasted 202 — which suggests the winning strategy
is not to pick the right eleven, but to own better players than everyone else and accept
the losses.

The single worst afternoon belonged to Peter Vickers, who left 30 points on the bench in
[GW26](gw:26).
